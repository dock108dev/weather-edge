# November 15 comparative paper research

The paper study uses the same $500 starting cash, $25 station-day exposure cap, and captured book tape for each rule. The development window ends at 12:07:28 p.m. EST on November 15. The forward window begins at that boundary. Both windows use the same climate date and some of the same stations. The settings grid varies a $10 or $25 order cap, a 5–60 second reaction delay, and a $0.25 or $0.75 minimum expected net return at each order cap. The comparison kept the predefined $25, 30-second, $0.25 setting because no development station-day had a verified payout at the cutoff.

The broker compares the first usable book received between the reaction target and 15 seconds later. It prices displayed size and scheduled taker fees and applies one chronological cash ledger. Positions retain collateral through the first verified exchange payout receipt. Drawdown values open positions at their acquisition cost. Price histories and midpoint forecasts never create fills.

## Freshness changes the evidence

The initial development tape has 57,381 receipts across five station-days, all on November 15. The old zero-Age rule accepted 18,646 and the origin-confirmed rule accepted 7,480. Among the development receipts, 48,557 explicitly say `HIT`. The old rule accepted 11,001 cache hits with zero or missing Age. The revised policy requires an affirmative origin fetch or synchronous revalidation, an exchange timestamp that passes monotonicity checks, and an open uncrossed ladder. The browser labels cache hits and unknown Age on current quotes. Existing saved runs retain the freshness policy they recorded. [Polymarket US book reference](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/markets/get-market-book), [HTTP Age semantics](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111.html#name-age), [Cloudflare cache response meanings](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cache/concepts/cache-responses/)

For the same 1,152 two-sided signal snapshots, the old policy found 1,146 30-second rechecks, 520 of them explicit `HIT` responses. The revised policy found 626, all `EXPIRED` origin fetches. It found zero at 5, 15, and 45 seconds and 544 at 60 seconds. The collector's median interval on that tape was 15.116 seconds per instrument. Responses advertised a 30-second cache lifetime. Those facts explain the repeating recheck pattern without implying better forecasts or prices at a slower reaction time. The replay counts missing rechecks, price or forecast changes, and missing liquidity independently.
