# Central Park daily-high model

Run from the repository root with `uv run python -m weather_edge.model`. The command retrieves the public archive records it needs, reuses compact station cards in `.data/nbm-cards/`, and writes `.data/model/model.joblib` and `.data/model/report.json`. Build the historical replay first to include matched market scores. The model artifact is a trusted local joblib file generated by this command.

## What the model uses

- The NOAA reference is the as-issued [NBM short-range station bulletin](https://vlab.noaa.gov/web/mdl/nbm-textcard-v5.0) for KNYC. `TXN` supplies the deterministic 18-hour maximum guidance. Its valid stamp is 00 UTC and its window is 12–06 UTC. The contract instead uses the official climate day from 00:00 to 24:00 local standard time. The model targets that official high. When a newer bulletin lacks a `TXN` for the climate day, the previous eligible `TXN` remains the model reference and the newer hourly guidance remains available to explore.
- NOAA's hourly NBH station guidance supplies the expected temperatures used to compare station readings. For paired forecasts the later NOAA S3 Last-Modified timestamp is the first replay availability. That timestamp records the archive object's publication. KNYC archive requests use HTTP byte ranges and verify the station and issue timestamp before caching.
- The [Iowa Environmental Mesonet ASOS archive](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/ASOS/) supplies routine and special station temperatures. Training and weather-only evaluation use observation time plus ten minutes because the archive rows lack the original ingest time. The Nov. 5 matched comparison uses the replay's verified observation timestamps. Observation features use only readings within the relevant fixed UTC−5 climate day and received by the decision time.
- The target is the integer maximum shown in the complete [NWS Central Park daily climate report](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/json/cli.py?station=KNYC&year=2026) after the climate day ends. Training and calibration each require the relevant reports to be published before the following split's first decision.

The scikit-learn pipeline vectorizes and standardizes the inputs and fits Ridge with α = 10 to official high minus `TXN`. Time and lead, the observed high relative to `TXN`, the latest reading's deviation from its hourly forecast, and the recent trend comparison enter the fit with missingness indicators. KNYC is the only fitted station. Its one-hot coefficient is zero.

Ridge fit a downward average residual of **−1.07°F** on August–early October. The strongest positive standardized coefficients were observed high relative to `TXN` (**+1.28°F per standard deviation**) and latest reading relative to NBH (**+0.61°F per standard deviation**). The observation-present indicator was **+1.04°F per standard deviation**. These regularized weights describe joint associations. In the later test period, the fit ran **0.97°F cold** on average vs **0.38°F cold** for raw NOAA.

In each time band, one averaged residual per prior validation date contributes to the uncertainty distribution. The residual shape is centered and smoothed with a 0.5°F Gaussian kernel. A bucket of 71–72°F therefore integrates the temperature distribution from 70.5°F to 72.5°F. Open ends include the corresponding full tail. Bucket estimates carry limited-support status when fewer than five prior validation residuals support either outcome, or when the calibration has fewer than eight independent dates. The baseline integrates a Gaussian centered on `TXN` using NOAA's as-issued `XND` spread, with a prior-validation spread fallback if needed.

The weather-only Brier score averages 588 bucket cases. Each climate day uses one fixed seven-bucket partition centered on that day's first available NOAA forecast used in the test. Five buckets span two integer degrees each, with both tails included. Adjacent snapshots use the same daily outcome and are correlated.

At 13:00 local standard time Ridge temperature MAE improved from 1.000°F to **0.790°F**, while bucket Brier worsened from 0.08908 to 0.10456. Its worst miss was the prior-evening Nov. 6 decision, when Ridge predicted **64.98°F**, NOAA guided **66°F** and the NWS reported **70°F**. On Nov. 12's prior-evening decision, Ridge predicted **57.88°F**, NOAA guided **59°F**, and the report showed **61°F**. The local report contains all decisions, the six worst date-level misses, and per-time-band scores.

A second nonlinear model needs more independent station dates to make the comparison informative. This run kept the Ridge fit fixed through the untouched test.

## Matched Polymarket US replay

The verified Nov. 5 event supplied five decisions at 03:00, 09:00, 13:00, 17:00, and 21:00 EST. The comparison uses all six real contract buckets at each decision and the latest YES display price available by that decision and labeled within the preceding 60 seconds, for 30 matched cases on **one independent day**. The archive's minute samples have a conservative one-minute availability lag because the source does not specify when within the minute each value became visible. Held-out Brier was **0.09032** for NOAA, **0.06460** for Ridge, and **0.16302** for the raw market YES display price. The winning 71–72°F bucket settled at 72°F. At 21:00 EST, the market display was 99¢, while Ridge assigned 57.86% and NOAA assigned 37.21%. The market showed much stronger confidence late in the day despite its higher aggregate Brier over these five timestamps.

These display-price comparisons have no captured depth for an execution replay. Repeated decisions on one settlement day do not establish a repeatable edge, and Ridge underperformed the baseline on the broader test.
